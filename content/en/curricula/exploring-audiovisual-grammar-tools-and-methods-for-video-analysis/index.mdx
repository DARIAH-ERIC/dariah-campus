## Introduction

Audiovisual works do not simply record the world. They organise images and sounds through choices of framing, duration, editing, point of view, rhythm and sound. These choices influence what viewers notice, how they connect images and how they interpret a scene.

*Exploring Audiovisual Grammar: Tools and Methods for Video Analysis* introduces six fundamental operations of audiovisual language. Each module is built around a short episode in which the same everyday situation is presented in five different ways. Comparing these variations makes it possible to identify particular formal choices and observe how they transform the viewer’s experience.

## What the course explores

The course invites learners to move from an initial impression towards a more precise analysis of audiovisual form. Through observation, comparison, collaborative annotation and practical experimentation, they learn to:

* identify key choices involving editing, framing, point of view, sound and sequencing;
* analyse how images and sounds organise time, space, attention and meaning;
* distinguish between what is shown, what is heard and what viewers are invited to imagine;
* explain how different formal choices transform the interpretation of the same action or situation.

The aim is not to establish a single correct interpretation, but to provide learners with a clear vocabulary and practical methods for understanding how audiovisual meaning is constructed.

## Course structure

The course consists of six complementary modules:

* **CUT — Editing, duration and rhythm:** how long takes, ellipses, matches, abrupt cuts and slow motion shape the experience of time.
* **OBSERVE — Framing, distance and point of view:** how camera position, framing and shot scale determine the viewer’s relationship to a scene.
* **HEAR — Sound, silence and points of audition:** how ambience, off-screen sounds, music, voice-over and bodily sounds construct space, direct attention and influence emotion.
* **CONNECT — Images, associations and sequencing:** how juxtaposition, repetition, sequence and visual clues encourage viewers to connect fragments and formulate hypotheses.
* **SHOW / HIDE — Framing, off-screen space and traces:** how sounds, objects, empty spaces and visible consequences make absent events perceptible.
* **CHOOSE — Selection, variation and decision-making:** how selecting, excluding, arranging and transforming audiovisual material produces different interpretations of the same situation.

## Who this course is for

The course is intended primarily for teachers, trainers, students and anyone interested in image education, audiovisual analysis or media literacy. It can also support researchers and professionals looking for practical methods with which to discuss audiovisual works.

No previous specialist knowledge of film or video production is required. The concepts are introduced progressively through observation and comparison before being applied in analytical and practical activities.

## How to use the course

The modules can be followed in sequence, with the final module bringing together the principles explored throughout the course. They can also be used independently when focusing on a particular aspect of audiovisual language.

Each module contains a short episode composed of five variations, a comparative analysis, explanations of the key concepts, three activities and suggestions for further viewing and reading. The activities move from collective observation to collaborative annotation and audiovisual production. They are designed primarily for use with a group or class, but their duration and level of difficulty can be adapted to the learners and the time available.
