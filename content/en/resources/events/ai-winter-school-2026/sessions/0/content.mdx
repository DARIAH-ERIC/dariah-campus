This two-part session by Christopher Pollin introduces vibe coding and Promptotyping, a structured approach to working with large language models through prompt and context engineering. Using examples from digital humanities and cultural heritage, learners explore model selection, tokenisation, context windows, data formats, code execution, and an introductory Python exercise with CSV data. The session also addresses confabulation, sycophancy, bias, privacy, and the need for continuous verification. Its central concept, asymmetric amplification, describes how AI can extend computer-based research while leaving expertise, interpretation, and responsibility with the researcher.

## Learning Outcomes

After watching this video, learners will be able to:

* Explain how large language models generate outputs and evaluate how model capabilities, limitations, access conditions, and tool interfaces affect their use in research.
* Apply Prompt Engineering and Context Engineering to formulate research tasks, structure relevant knowledge, and manage the information available to a large language model.
* Develop and verify a functional research prototype using Vibe Coding and Promptotyping, translating research questions and domain expertise into requirements and iterative implementation.
* Design and critically evaluate agentic research workflows by specifying agent roles, delegation boundaries, and verification checkpoints while assessing their implications for research integrity, accountability, unequal access, and resource use.
