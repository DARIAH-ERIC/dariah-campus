This two-part session by Christopher Pollin introduces vibe coding and Promptotyping, a structured approach to working with large language models through prompt and context engineering. Using examples from digital humanities and cultural heritage, learners explore model selection, tokenisation, context windows, data formats, code execution, and an introductory Python exercise with CSV data. The session also addresses confabulation, sycophancy, bias, privacy, and the need for continuous verification. Its central concept, **asymmetric amplification**, describes how AI can extend computer-based research while leaving expertise, interpretation, and responsibility with the researcher.

### **Learning Outcomes**

After completing this resource, learners will be able to:

* **Explain and critically evaluate how large language models generate outputs**, including their capabilities, limitations, access conditions, interfaces, and implications for research integrity, accountability, unequal access, and resource use.
* **Apply Prompt Engineering and Context Engineering to research tasks**, structuring relevant knowledge, managing model context, and translating research questions and domain expertise into clear requirements.
* **Develop, verify, and evaluate functional AI-assisted research prototypes and workflows** using Vibe Coding, Promptotyping, and agentic approaches, including the definition of agent roles, delegation boundaries, and verification checkpoints.
