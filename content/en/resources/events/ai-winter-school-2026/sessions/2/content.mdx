This talk offers a rigorous yet accessible introduction to the topic of bias in machine learning systems. It begins with a concise overview of the fundamentals of machine learning, covering key concepts such as model training, learning paradigms, and typical areas of application in contemporary ML approaches. Building on this foundation, the talk examines different forms of social and technical bias that may arise, particularly in modern deep learning applications.

Special attention is given to the real-world implications of such biases. In addition to societal risks and potential forms of discrimination, the talk also addresses technical consequences, including limited generalizability, inaccurate predictions, and reduced model reliability. It concludes by presenting practical strategies for identifying, assessing, and mitigating bias, thereby supporting a more responsible and reflective use of machine learning technologies.

## Target Audience

This talk is designed for a broad audience and does not require extensive prior knowledge of machine learning. It is particularly suitable for:

&#x20;

* beginners and non-experts
* data scientists and machine learning practitioners
* product managers and decision-makers
* researchers, educators, and professionals
* anyone interested in the societal impact and responsible use of machine learning and deep learning technologies

## Learning Outcomes

After watching this video, learners will be able to:

* understand basic principles of how modern deep learning methods work by learning from data.
* define and distinguish between common sources and types of bias in machine learning.
* critically evaluate ethical, social, and legal consequences of deploying biased AI systems.
* understand how biased data and models lead to skewed model outputs and real-world impacts (e.g., in healthcare, justice systems, hiring, etc.).
* learn about techniques for assessing and reducing bias in machine learning systems.
