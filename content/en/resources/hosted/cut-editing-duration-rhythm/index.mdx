## Introduction

### Cutting: editing as a way of shaping time

In this first module, audiovisual language is approached through a fundamental operation: cutting. To cut is not simply to remove part of an action or to move from one shot to another. It means organising time: deciding what remains visible, what disappears, what connects to what, and what is compressed or stretched.

The episode is based on a very simple situation: a couple unpacking boxes in an apartment. The action is ordinary, but it is repeated several times through different editing approaches. The module therefore invites learners to distinguish between real time, filmed time and perceived time. Audiovisual language does not merely record time: it shapes it.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this module, you will be able to :

* understand that editing constructs an experience of time;
* identify several ways of structuring time: long take, ellipsis, match, hard cut and slow motion;
* distinguish between actual continuity and the impression of continuity;
* analyse the effects produced by cuts on the viewer’s perception;
* put into words sensations of duration, interruption, acceleration or slowing down;
* use collaborative annotation to identify the moments when time shifts.

## Episode analysis

### Same action, several experiences of time

The episode is based on a simple action: a couple unpacking boxes in an apartment. This ordinary situation serves as a starting point for exploring editing. The aim is not to tell a story, but to make viewers feel that audiovisual time is always constructed.

The scene is repeated according to five temporal modes: continuous, hollowed-out, gapped, smoothed and stretched time. The action remains identifiable, but perception changes. Audiovisual form does not only modify duration; it also transforms the way we understand and feel the action.

In this module, audiovisual form appears as a way of shaping time. Cutting is not only moving from one image to another. It means constructing a duration, organising continuity, producing a rupture or transforming the viewer’s attention. Editing acts directly on the experience of time.

### Five variations

<Callout kind="none" title="Explore five variations">
  <Disclosure title="Continuous time">
    The first variation shows the action in apparent continuity. It establishes a point of reference: the viewer observes an ordinary duration.&#x20;

    **To observe:** Continuity of the action, the impression of real duration, and the role of objects as markers of time.&#x20;

    **Concepts:** Long take, duration, continuity.
  </Disclosure>

  <Disclosure title="Hollowed-out time">
    Fragments of time disappear. The action remains understandable, but the viewer must mentally reconstruct what is missing.

    **To observe:** Changes of state, missing segments, and what is understood without being shown.

    **Concepts:** Ellipsis, cut, the viewer’s reconstruction
  </Disclosure>

  <Disclosure title="Grapped time">
    The cuts become visible and abrupt. The action moves forward in jumps: time is condensed and punctured.

    **To observe:**&#x48;ard cuts, temporal jumps, and the effects of acceleration and discontinuity.

    **Concepts:** Hard cut, jump cut, fragmented rhythm.
  </Disclosure>

  <Disclosure title="Smoothed time">
    The action is fragmented into several shots, but the cuts are made discreet through smooth continuity matches.

    **To observe:** Gestures or objects that ensure continuity and create an impression of fluidity.

    **Concepts:** Match, invisible editing, classical continuity.
  </Disclosure>

  <Disclosure title="Stretched time">
    Ordinary gestures are slowed down. The viewer perceives the gesture’s duration, texture and presence.

    **To observe:** Slowing down, an impression of suspension, and the new value given to the ordinary.

    **Concepts:** Slow motion, temporal dilation, attention to gesture.
  </Disclosure>
</Callout>

### What the episode explores

* **The time of the action:** The time needed to unpack boxes, move objects and organise the space.
* **The time created by editing:** The time constructed through the arrangement of shots: what is kept, removed, connected, accelerated or slowed down.
* **The time perceived by the viewer:** The time felt by the viewer: shorter, longer, more fluid, more fragmented or denser.

**Audiovisual language does not reproduce time as it is. It creates a temporal experience.**

## Key concepts

* **Editing:** Organisation of shots within a specific duration and order.
* **Cut:** The transition from one shot to another. It may be visible, discreet, abrupt, smooth or expressive.
* **Long take:** A single extended shot that gives the impression of an action unfolding in continuous time.
* **Ellipsis:** The omission of a segment of time that the viewer mentally reconstructs.
* **Match:** A visual or sound connection between two shots, used to create continuity or a transition effect.
* **Rhythm:** The perceptible organisation of durations, cuts, repetitions, accelerations and suspensions.
