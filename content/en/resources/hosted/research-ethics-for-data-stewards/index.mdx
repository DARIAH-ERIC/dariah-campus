This session briefly explores research ethics and the role data stewards play in supporting responsible research practice. It introduces core principles of research and data ethics, explains why ethics matters beyond formal approval, and examines how ethical considerations arise across the entire research data lifecycle. Through practical examples and common dilemmas such as consent limitations, data sharing risks, FAIR data, and reuse we will consider how data stewardship can aid everyday decision making. 

We also hear from Dr Claire O'Neill in her role as Chair of UCC's Social Research Ethics Committee. She outlines some of the core principles of research ethics and why having ethical approval is important for research.&#x20;

## Learning Outcomes&#x20;

1. Explain what research ethics is and why it matters.​
2. Identify the core principles of research ethics.​
3. Describe how ethical principles apply specifically to research data.
4. Articulate the responsibilities of a data steward in promoting ethical research practice.​
5. Recognise some common ethical risks in data stewardship and consider how to mitigate them

## Reflective Exercise - Research Ethics&#x20;

<Callout kind="none" title="But it's public data' - the ethics of using public data">
  Please read the two papers below and reflect on the questions. Please consider the topic from a data stewards perspective.&#x20;

  * **Lewis et al. (2008), “Taste, Ties, and Time: A New Social Network Dataset Using Facebook.com”**[https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378873308000385](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378873308000385)
  * **Zimmer, M. (2010), “‘But the Data is Already Public’: On the Ethics of Research Using Facebook Data”**[https://www.sfu.ca/\~palys/Zimmer-2010-EthicsOfResearchFromFacebook.pdf](https://www.sfu.ca/~palys/Zimmer-2010-EthicsOfResearchFromFacebook.pdf)

  According to Zimmer,  “public availability”  is not enough to justify ethical use.

  1. &#x20;In your opinion is it ethically permissible for researchers to use publicly accessible social‑media data without users' consent?&#x20;
  2. As a data steward, where do you locate the *ethical failure* (if any)?&#x20;
  3. If you were responsible for  stewardship of this dataset **today**, what would you do differently?
</Callout>
