This resource introduces the application of 3D scanning technologies in the preservation of Icelandic turf houses and discusses how digital methods are combined with museum conservation to safeguard at-risk cultural heritage, highlighting the use of long-range LiDAR scanning and point cloud analysis.  Particular emphasis is placed on how non-invasive 3D scanning enables the measurement and monitoring of structural displacement within timber frames over time, while also underlining the importance of accounting for environmental and hydrothermal factors to inform targeted conservation strategies and ensure the long-term survival of vernacular architecture.\
\
This presentation is part of the *Digital Humanities After Hours* lecture series, hosted by the Centre for Digital Humanities and Arts (DARIAH-IS/CDHA, [https://www.mshl.is](https://www.mshl.is/)) in Autumn 2025.

## Learning Outcomes

After studying the resource, learners should be able to:

* explain the structural and environmental vulnerabilities of vernacular turf architecture, identifying key conservation challenges;
* evaluate non-destructive 3D scanning and remote sensing techniques used to analyze geometrical changes and monitor structural health in historical timber and turf buildings;
* assess the impact of hygrothermal fluctuations on organic building materials over long-term monitoring cycles;
* formulate digital documentation strategies for remote heritage management, understanding how quantitative 3D data enables heritage institutions to prioritize preventive maintenance across distributed sites.
