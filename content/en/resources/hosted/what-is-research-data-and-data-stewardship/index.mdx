## Introduction

In this unit we look at **research data stewardship** and why it is essential to high-quality, ethical, and impactful research. It explores what counts as research data (and what doesn’t), the many forms data can take across disciplines, and how data evolves through a dynamic research data lifecycle. The session highlights the role of data stewardship in supporting good research practice — from early planning and data collection to documentation, secure storage, FAIR sharing, and long-term preservation.

These presentations also demystifies who data stewards are, what they do (and don’t do), and the different models of data stewardship found in research environments, including central, embedded, technical, and hybrid approaches. Through real-world examples and cautionary case studies, it shows how poor data stewardship can undermine research integrity, reuse, and trust, while effective stewardship enables reproducibility, compliance, innovation, and open research. One key takeaway is that data stewardship is a shared responsibility across the research ecosystem, that it focuses on enablement rather than control.

## Learning Outcomes

1. Describing the role of data in research and its underpinning nature in the Research Life Cycle.
2. Recognise at least one high level abstraction model for the life cycle of a research project (eg. prepare, collect, analyse, disseminate, share).
3. Recognise the link between data stewardship and good research practice and integrity as defined by national/international Code of Conducts and possible norms and requirements within the specific field of research.
4. Identifying the main motivations for implementing good data  stewardship among different stakeholders around research.&#x20;
5. Identifying and explaining the different models of data stewardship, such that the role is well understood and defined within the context of the institution and research; Generic & Advisory, Embedded & Operational and Policy, Strategy & Coordination.
