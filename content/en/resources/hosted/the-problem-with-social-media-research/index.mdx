  content: >-
    This ACDH lecture by Richard Rogers focuses on the methodological, ethical
    and epistemological problems of social media research. Rather than
    criticising social media itself, Rogers examines how researchers use social
    media platforms as data sources and why this practice is highly problematic.
    He discusses the instability of social media data, changing metrics,
    platform rules, deleted content, limited archives and corporate control over
    access to data. The lecture also addresses the role of human subjects,
    contextual privacy and the questionable assumption that publicly available
    posts can automatically be used for research. Rogers further reflects on how
    commercial platform logics, algorithmic optimisation and engagement-driven
    design shape the data researchers analyse. By discussing alternatives such
    as digital ethnography, small data approaches, scraping, alternative
    platforms, GDPR and the Digital Services Act, the lecture highlights the
    need for stronger ethical reflection and argues that ethics should be
    treated not merely as compliance, but as a foundational part of digital
    research practice.
