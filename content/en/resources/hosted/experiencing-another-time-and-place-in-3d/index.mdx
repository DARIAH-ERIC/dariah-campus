This resource presents a digital humanities project that brings to life a historic 200-kilometer postal route between Vopnafjörður and Kelduhverfi in northern Iceland using interactive 3D technology. Developed by a research team at Reykjavík University in collaboration with the postal history museum at Ásbrandsstaðir, the initiative transforms open-source geological and natural datasets into an immersive virtual environment. Particular emphasis is placed on how geospatial data visualization and 3D modeling can reconstruct historical landscapes and physical journeys across challenging terrain, also underlining the potential of partnerships between universities and museums in creating engaging, accessible digital heritage experiences.\
\
This presentation is part of the *Lunch with* *Digital Humanities* (LwDH) lecture series, hosted by the Centre for Digital Humanities and Arts (DARIAH-IS/CDHA, [https://www.mshl.is](https://www.mshl.is/)) in Spring 2026.

## Learning Outcomes

After studying the resource, learners should be able to:

* evaluate the procedural trade-offs between 360-degree video, LiDAR point clouds, satellite elevation models, and procedural VR environments;
* understand procedural landscape rendering using ecological habitat datasets combined with depth-encoded materials;
* implement dynamic spatial audio algorithms that instantiate localized bird calls and soundscapes;
* Integrate oral history interviews, architectural fieldwork, 3D artifact scans and archival photography into real-time 3D space.
