## Introduction

### Observe: framing, distance, point of view

In this second module, audiovisual language is approached through an apparently simple gesture: observing. To observe is not simply to look at what is in front of us. It always means looking from a position, through a frame, with a certain distance, a certain proximity and a certain orientation of the gaze.

The episode is based on an ordinary situation: a couple assembling a piece of furniture in an otherwise empty living room. The action remains broadly the same, but it is filmed through several modes of looking: documentary, subjective, fragmented, object-based and selfie. The viewer never sees exactly the same scene, even when the situation does not change.

The module therefore invites learners to distinguish between what is shown, the position from which it is shown, and the effect produced by that position of looking. Audiovisual language does not merely give access to a scene: it organises a way of observing it.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

* understand that point of view transforms the perception of a scene;
* identify several modes of looking: documentary, subjective, fragmented, object-based and selfie;
* distinguish between framing, distance, shot scale and camera position;
* analyse the effects produced by proximity, distance or fragmentation;
* use collaborative annotation to identify the moments when the position of the gaze changes the viewer’s experience.

## Key concepts

* **Frame:** The boundary of the image. The frame determines what is visible, what is excluded and how elements are organised.
* **Composition:** The organisation of bodies, objects, lines, empty spaces and movements within the frame.
* **Point of view:** The position from which a scene is shown. It may be physical, perceptual, narrative or affective.
* **Point-of-view shot:** A shot in which the camera adopts a character’s viewpoint, as if the viewer were seeing through their eyes.
* **Shot scale:** The apparent distance between the camera and the subject: wide shot, medium shot, close-up or extreme close-up.
* **Fragmentation:** The division of a scene into partial visual fragments: hands, objects, faces or details. The viewer mentally reconstructs the whole.
