## Introduction

### Observe: framing, distance, point of view

In this second module, audiovisual language is approached through an apparently simple gesture: observing. To observe is not simply to look at what is in front of us. It always means looking from a position, through a frame, with a certain distance, a certain proximity and a certain orientation of the gaze.

The episode is based on an ordinary situation: a couple assembling a piece of furniture in an otherwise empty living room. The action remains broadly the same, but it is filmed through several contrasting modes of looking. The viewer never sees exactly the same scene, even when the situation does not change.

The module therefore invites learners to distinguish between what is shown, the position from which it is shown, and the effect produced by that position of looking. Audiovisual language does not merely give access to a scene: it organises a way of observing it.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

* understand that point of view transforms the perception of a scene;
* identify several modes of looking: documentary, subjective, fragmented, object-based and selfie;
* distinguish between framing, distance, shot scale and camera position;
* analyse the effects produced by proximity, distance or fragmentation;
* use collaborative annotation to identify the moments when the position of the gaze changes the viewer’s experience.

## Watch the episode

Watch the episode once without taking notes. Pay attention to how each mode of looking changes your relationship to the same scene. From what position do we observe the action? When do you feel close to it, distant from it, immersed in it or excluded from it?

## Episode analysis

### Comparing five ways of looking

The five variations organise the same action through contrasting viewing positions: documentary, subjective, fragmented, object-based and selfie. Comparing them reveals how framing, shot scale, camera position and distance shape the viewer’s relationship to the scene. The action remains recognisable, but each visual choice offers a different way of observing it.

### Five variations

<Callout kind="none" title="Explore the five variations">
  <Disclosure title="Documentary gaze">
    The scene is observed from a distance, with a static camera. The couple appears as a phenomenon to be described.

    **To observe:** Frontality, stability of the frame, distance of observation.

    **Concepts:** Static camera, wide framing, observational distance.
  </Disclosure>

  <Disclosure title="Subjective gaze">
    The scene is perceived from the position of one of the characters. The viewer shares a situated place within the interaction.

    **To observe:** Point-of-view shot, imperfect framing, proximity and orientation of the gaze.

    **Concepts:** Subjective viewpoint, point-of-view shot, visual immersion.
  </Disclosure>

  <Disclosure title="Fragmented gaze">
    The action is presented in pieces: hands, objects, faces and details. The viewer must mentally reconstruct the whole.

    **To observe:** Absence of an establishing shot, extreme close-ups and reconstruction of the action.

    **Concepts:** Fragmentation, detail, off-screen space.
  </Disclosure>

  <Disclosure title="Object-based gaze">
    The camera adopts the position of the box. The gaze seems to be located within the material object itself.

    **To observe:** Unusual point of view, handling and movements of the object.

    **Concepts:** Object-based viewpoint, embedded camera, unusual camera position.
  </Disclosure>

  <Disclosure title="Selfie gaze">
    The couple films itself with a smartphone. The scene becomes a small self-mediated event.

    **To observe:** Handheld camera, unstable framing and gaze towards the screen.

    **Concepts:** Self-representation, self-mediated image, direct address.
  </Disclosure>
</Callout>

### What the episode explores

* **What is shown:** The same action provides a shared basis for comparing the five variations.
* **The position of the gaze:** The camera successively occupies the position of an outside observer, a character, a fragmented viewpoint, an object or a smartphone held by the couple.
* **The effect produced:** The scene may feel distant, immersive, fragmented, strange, playful or self-conscious.

**Key takeaway**

> Audiovisual language does not simply show a scene. Through framing, distance and camera position, it proposes a place from which the viewer observes and experiences it.

## Key concepts

The following sections examine two complementary dimensions of observation. Point of view organises the viewer’s position in relation to a scene, while framing, distance and composition organise what becomes visible within the image.

<Callout kind="none" title="Explore the key concepts">
  <Disclosure title="Point of view: organising a place for the viewer">
    An image is always situated. Every image is produced from a position: a distance, a height, an orientation, a frame and a relationship to bodies and objects. Point of view does not only correspond to the physical location of the camera. It also determines how the scene becomes perceptible: near or distant, intimate or external, stable or unstable, partial or overall.

    **Four dimensions of point of view**

    * **Physical point of view:** The material place from which the scene is filmed: in front, behind, above, at eye level, close to the ground, inside an object or in a character’s hand. It answers the question: Where is the camera placed?
    * **Perceptual point of view:** The way the image organises what the viewer can perceive. The frame may provide a wide view, isolate a detail, hide part of the action or direct attention. It answers the question: What does this position allow us to see or prevent us from seeing?
    * **Narrative point of view:** The relationship between what the viewer sees and what the characters know, see or ignore. It answers the question: With whom do we see, or according to what logic is the scene presented?
    * **Affective point of view:** The effect produced by the position of looking: proximity, distance, discomfort, immersion, strangeness, complicity or humour. It answers the question: What does this way of looking make us feel?


  </Disclosure>
