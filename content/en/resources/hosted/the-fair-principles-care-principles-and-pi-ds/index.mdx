This session introduces the FAIR Principles, Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable, and explores how they strengthen research transparency, reproducibility, and long‑term data value. We’ll look at what FAIR really means in practice, from metadata and persistent identifiers to repository selection.

Through discipline‑specific examples, we’ll examine how FAIR needs to be adapted across research domains and highlight the essential partnership between researchers and data stewards. The session also showcases key tools, infrastructures, and practical actions you can build into a Data Management Plan to support FAIR implementation. We will also consider the CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance, Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, and Ethics, and explore how CARE complements FAIR by emphasising the rights, interests, and governance of communities represented in research data.

## Learning Outcomes&#x20;

* Explain the FAIR Principles and their importance in research data management.​
* Describe the characteristics of Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable data.​
* Identify the role of data stewards in supporting FAIR data practices.​&#x20;
* Recognise tools and infrastructures that support FAIR data.​&#x20;
* Apply a FAIR assessment tool to evaluate a dataset.
