This session introduces the FAIR Principles, Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable, and explores how they strengthen research transparency, reproducibility, and long‑term data value. We’ll look at what FAIR really means in practice, from metadata and persistent identifiers to repository selection.

Through discipline‑specific examples, we’ll examine how FAIR needs to be adapted across research domains and highlight the essential partnership between researchers and data stewards. The session also showcases key tools, infrastructures, and practical actions you can build into a Data Management Plan to support FAIR implementation. We will also consider the CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance, Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, and Ethics, and explore how CARE complements FAIR by emphasising the rights, interests, and governance of communities represented in research data.

## Learning Outcomes&#x20;

* Explain the FAIR Principles and their importance in research data management.​
* Describe the characteristics of Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable data.​
* Identify the role of data stewards in supporting FAIR data practices.​&#x20;
* Recognise tools and infrastructures that support FAIR data.​&#x20;
* Apply a FAIR assessment tool to evaluate a dataset.

## Self-Reflection Exercise&#x20;



<Callout kind="note" title="How FAIR are the Data?">
  Select one dataset from the list provided below and evaluate it using one FAIR assessment tools also listed below. Consider the following questions:

  * How well the dataset meets the FAIR principles
  * Specific strengths and weaknesses you observed in the dataset
  * Do you have enough information to reuse the data
  * Any limitations of the dataset or the assessment tool

  **Datasets**

  1. **St. Kieran's College Class Portraits dataset:** Richard Fitzpatrick. (2022) St. Kieran's College, Kilkenny, Class Portraits and Student Records Digitisation Project 2021, Digital Repository of Ireland \[Distributor], Royal Irish Academy \[Depositing Institution], [https://doi.org/10.7486/DRI.xg94xg33g.](https://doi.org/10.7486/DRI.xg94xg33g)
  2. **Research Data Stewardship Survey - University College Cork,** This survey aimed to help us gain an understanding of research data stewardship activities in UCC, the scope of those activities, identify any gaps in current resources and skills and work out where the Research Data Service fits with related roles and services [https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.6912811](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.6912811)
  3. **Irish Women at Work Oral History Project,** interviews focused on the working lives of women (1930-1960) living in three counties of Ireland (Cork, Kerry and Limerick), [https://doi.org/10.7486/DRI.h9904j002](https://doi.org/10.7486/DRI.h9904j002)
  4. **Influence of beaver mimicry restoration on habitat availability for fishes, including Arctic grayling (Thymallus arcticus)** Beaver-dam-mimicry is an emergent conservation practice. We evaluated the influence of constructed riffles, a unique type of beaver mimicry aimed to store water and allow fish passage, on habitat for fishes in one control reach and one manipulated reach with mimicry structures added. [https://zenodo.org/record/5839189#.YeANoP7P2Uk](https://zenodo.org/record/5839189#.YeANoP7P2Uk)  
  5. **Vibrant Clean Energy Renewable Generation Profiles**  This dataset was produced by Vibrant Clean Energy, and is licensed to the public under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license (CC-BY-4.0). The data consists of hourly, county-level renewable generation profiles in the continental United States and was compiled based on outputs from the NOAA HRRR weather model. [https://zenodo.org/records/13920120](https://zenodo.org/records/13920120)
  6. Data supporting "**A comprehensive analysis of air-sea CO2 flux uncertainties constructed from surface ocean data products**" [https://zenodo.org/records/13911533](https://zenodo.org/records/13911533)
