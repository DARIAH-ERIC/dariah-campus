This resource presents the *Kvennaspor: Unearthing and Foregrounding Women in Icelandic Saga Landscapes* project, demonstrating how Feminist Digital Humanities frameworks recover and foreground women in medieval narrative sources and historical travel literature. The talk outlines three core research strands: analyzing spatial placement of female characters in the Sagas of Icelanders, uncovering accounts of women traveling to Iceland, and engaging in practice-as-research community co-creation. The lecture addresses critical theoretical themes including knowledge representation, algorithmic bias, and archival erasure in digital systems. Technically, the project highlights sustainable data reuse by integrating with existing infrastructures such as the <Link link={{"discriminant":"external","value":"https://sagamap.hi.is/is/"}}>Icelandic Saga Map</Link> and [Nafnið.is](https://nafnið.is/) to enable georeferenced text mapping, network visualization and FAIR data alignment across cultural heritage resources.

\
This presentation is part of the *Digital Humanities After Hours* lecture series, hosted by the Centre for Digital Humanities and Arts (DARIAH-IS/CDHA, [https://www.mshl.is](https://www.mshl.is/)) in Autumn 2025.

## Learning Outcomes

After studying the resource, learners should be able to:

* evaluate how feminist theory and critical data studies inform database architecture, data modeling and spatial humanities design;
* analyze strategies for identifying, modeling, and foregrounding women's presence, agency and spatial associations in historical and literary sources;
* examine methods for linking disparate geographical, literary and onomastic datasets such as place-name registries and mapping infrastructures to address archival silences.
