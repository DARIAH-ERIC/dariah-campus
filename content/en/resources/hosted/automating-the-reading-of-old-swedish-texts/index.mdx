This resource presents the development of the first Automated Text Recognition (ATR) model for Old Swedish handwritten texts from the early 14th to the mid-16th century within the eScriptorium environment, created as part of the project Digital Approaches to the Survival and Loss of Old Norse Romances. Building on Bifrost, an open-access HTR model for Old Norse, the work details the creation of ground-truth datasets using digital manuscript surrogates from [manuscripta.se](https://manuscripta.se/) and <Link link={{"discriminant":"external","value":"https://handrit.is/"}}>handrit.is.</Link> Particular emphasis is placed on addressing methodological challenges such as palaeographic variation, transcription conventions and benchmark construction from scratch, while also underlining best practices for integrating ATR workflows into medieval Scandinavian philology.\
\
This presentation is part of the *Lunch with* *Digital Humanities* (LwDH) lecture series, hosted by the Centre for Digital Humanities and Arts (DARIAH-IS/CDHA, [https://www.mshl.is](https://www.mshl.is/)) in Spring 2026.

## Learning Outcomes

After studying the resource, learners should be able to:

* evaluate the application of Handwritten Text Recognition (HTR) workflows and the BIFROST incremental training protocol to medieval East Norse manuscript corpora;
* understand layout segmentation and ground truth preparation using open-source virtual research environments like eScriptorium, SegmOnto and Kraken;
* apply ecological statistical models such as unseen species estimations to quantify manuscript loss across historical literary traditions;
* develop FAIR-compliant data publication and workflow strategies for under-resourced medieval language traditions.
