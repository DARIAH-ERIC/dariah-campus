## Introduction

This training module helps you to make your dataset of bibliographic data more accessible and reusable via creating a human- and machine-readable documentation.

## Learning Outcomes

After completing the training module learners will be able to:&#x20;

* Describe what a datasheet is.
* Explain why documentation is important when publishing a collection of bibliographic data.
* Fill out the datasheet template alongside creating a bibliodata collection.
* Understand what are the mandatory, recommended and desirable fields in a datasheet.
* Combine planning and managing a research project and documentation.

## What is a Datasheet and Why Do We Need It?

You know already that bibliographic data is metadata given in a specified schema, collected in some sort of catalogue and always bound to a book or any other object stored and described in a (digital) library, museum, archive or repository.

<Callout kind="none">
  **Do you really need something like a datasheet to describe your bibliodata collection?**
</Callout>

<Callout kind="none">
  **The answer is: Yes - because you want people to understand and reuse your data!**
</Callout>

<Callout kind="important">
  A datasheet is a formal document which is human- and machine-readable. It should accompany a dataset or a data collection like an extended form of metadata.
</Callout>

### Context

The idea of a datasheets was first born in the context of the "Collections as Data" community and a Europeana working group in the Digital Cultural Heritage community.<Footnote>(see: Alkemade, H, et al. “Datasheets for Digital Cultural Heritage Datasets”. *Journal of Open Humanities Data*, vol. 9, no. 1, 2023, p. 17. [DOI: 10.5334/johd.124](https://doi.org/10.5334/johd.124)).</Footnote>

Coming up with a fixed template for a specific use case the Euopeana working group they achieve mostly two main things:&#x20;

1. comparability of collections and&#x20;
2. to provide a place for contextualisation (both for machines and humans).&#x20;

It is more flexible than a metadata schema but still an agreement on most important fields of information.

### The Datasheet

A datasheet is structured information about **where** the data is accessible and **how**, about the **content** (description, composition, people involved), the **process** (of creating, analyzing and handling), the **provenance** (ownership, rights, versions) and a longterm plan for **maintenance**, **reuse** and possible **ethical considerations** of a dataset or a collection of datasets.

Not all of the above mentioned categories are necessarily applicable to your data. Still, it is a recognizable effort which needs to be planned and done.

<Disclosure title="Where did I Put it? And How did I Do it?">
  After you did all the effort of creating and analysing your dataset, you probably run out of time, money and energy and the last thing on your mind is now filling out a formal document like a datasheet.
</Disclosure>

That's why it might be a good idea to start *before* and *during* the dataset creation. Like documentation for yourself and others should always be done naturally and even ritually on the side line: like brushing teeth in the morning and evening - the datasheet can be created alongside the dataset itself.

There are of course many different ways how to document. You can use a DMP-template (Data Management Plan) or the datasheet template in a simple pad or you decide for a project plan tool with a web interface. Wether you decide to call it a DMP, datasheet, Readme or any other kind of documentation it up to you. Either way, keep in mind of how your documentation will be provided and published long-term.

In the following chapters, you will learn how to fill out the individual sections of a datasheet, independantly of the tool you use in the end.

## **A Datasheet as Documentation for Bibliographic Datasets**

If you are using bibliographic data for your own research project, you know already that this kind of data was initially not created with research in mind.\
Bibliographic data originates from library catalogues, from book sales catalogues or (printed) bibliographies with a thematic focus. Usually it is highly structured data, following a metadata standard and / or a citation standard.\
Libarians or other field experts are particularly trained in creating and curating these datasets. They follow stricts guidelines and know their way through many different terminologies, ontologies and controlled vocabularies.\
When you as a researcher started to collect and create your dataset of bibliographic data you might have wished that an overview and high-level documentation exists. Many get lost in the labyrinth of library catalogues.

<Callout kind="tip">
  For your own dataset, make sure to provide this kind of documentation!
</Callout>

### **What does that mean for re-use and documentation?**

You want to encourage and enable re-use from various angles and backgrounds.\
For an easy start think about your fellow researchers: What do they need to know in order to re-use your bibliographic dataset?

The answer can vary from dataset to dataset.

In the following chapters, we'll go through the parts of the template and explain how you can fill out / document alongside creating a bibliodataset. We concentrate on mandatory and desirable fields.

### Mandatory Fields in the Datasheet

The **title** and the **description** can be filled in even before you start your research project. They are usually also mandatory for each research proposal or your DMP (Data Management Plan).

For the future publication of your dataset, think about the various compositions. You might want to publish an additional variant with a less project specific focus. Say, you analysed Spanish avant-garde journals of the 20th century and collected their bibliographic data via several  Spanish digital libraries and created initially a much broader defined dataset, it might be useful to provide both, your project specific collection and the broader dataset which is also not available in one single library and did cost you effort to create.

#### Description

The template says:

> Provide a brief descriptive overview of the dataset (‘at a glance’). DCAT-AP:M Briefly summarise the dataset, its intended use and the supported tasks. Give an overview of the contents of the dataset (data and metadata, only data or metadata; relating to physical, digitised or born-digital objects, etc.) how and why the dataset was created. The summary should describe the domain, topic, genre covered, keywords, and other relevant metadata. Clearly articulate the reasons for creating the dataset and promote transparency about funding interests. For what purpose was the dataset created? Was there a specific task in mind? Was there a specific gap that needed to be filled? Who created the dataset (e.g., which team, research group) and on behalf of which entity (e.g., company, institution, organisation)? Who funded the creation of the dataset?

For bibiodatasets concentrate on the context information: what is the scope of the dataset and the research question to which it was used to answer.

Information about the creation process, the people involved and the funding can be given in dedicated fields later.

#### Data Access URL

This field can only be filled after publishing or if you publish your datasheet and the data together, you can reserve a **DOI** beforehand in some repositories (e. g. Zenodo).

### Desirable Fields in the Datasheet

![](/assets/content/assets/en/resources/hosted/documentation-and-datasheets-for-bibliodata-resources/image.png)

#### The Publisher

In most cases this would be the name of the data repository or digital library which provides the persistent identifier for the dataset. It is important because of institutional responsibility (see maintenance plan)

#### Dataset Curators

The template says

> \[List the key people involved in collecting the dataset and their affiliation(s). Dataset curators are encouraged to insert some information about their positionality here, e.g. in the form of a short bio containing information on their formation, role and function within the CHI or project and the task(s) with which they were entrusted during the creation of the dataset.]

If you are a researcher and you created the bibliodataset yourself for a specific project, name yourself here. Additionally, there might have been more people involved who took care of the data publication etc. This field can be identical to the contact information field which might or might not be provided.

![Structure of the first part of the datasheet fields](/assets/content/assets/en/resources/hosted/documentation-and-datasheets-for-bibliodata-resources/image.png) &#x20;

#### Licencing Information, File Format and Citation Information

All three are essential and even mandatory in most of the data repositories.

If you are free to decide (without any given legal restrictions), bibliographic data should be provided under the free **licence** CC0 - public domain - or if you did contribute intellectually to the content itself you might use CC BY 4.0.<Footnote> [https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) and [https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/deed.en](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/deed.en)</Footnote>
