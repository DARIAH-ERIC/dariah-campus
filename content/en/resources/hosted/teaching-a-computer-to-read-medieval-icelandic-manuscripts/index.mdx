This resource presents initial results from a project aiming to develop the first open-access FAIR <Link link={{"discriminant":"external","value":"https://zenodo.org/records/15366896"}}>ATR model for medieval Old Norse-Icelandic manuscripts</Link>. Using only a small number of training pages to fine-tune the CATMuS-medieval model, the project achieved clear improvements in both in-domain and out-of-domain accuracy. These results demonstrate that effective models can be created with minimal data and without expensive hardware, as all experiments were carried out on a standard laptop. This lecture addresses the challenge posed by the limited availability of training data and introduces a workflow that enables low-cost fine-tuning of existing generic models for specific research purposes. By analysing the main types of recognition errors—particularly those involving special characters central to Old Norse palaeography—the implications for current transcription practices and the challenge of reconciling differing editorial conventions in the field are presented.\
\
This presentation is part of the *Digital Humanities After Hours* lecture series, hosted by the Centre for Digital Humanities and Arts (DARIAH-IS/CDHA, [https://www.mshl.is](https://www.mshl.is/)) in Autumn 2025.

## Learning Outcomes

After studying the resource, learners should be able to:

* formulate a low-resource fine-tuning pipeline using eScriptorium and Kraken to lower Character Error Rates with minimal training data;
* assess paleographic and graphemic trade-offs when modeling specialized characters versus normalized letter shapes;
* implement open science protocols by publishing ATR ground truth datasets and models on open repositories following FAIR principles;
* utilize automated text predictions for downstream digital humanities applications, including scribal hand clustering, large-scale corpus linguistics, and automated witness alignment for digital scholarly editions.
