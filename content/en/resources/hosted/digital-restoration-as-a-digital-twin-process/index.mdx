This resource is based on the presentation by Georgios Papaioannou (University of Athens) on digital restoration, virtual anastylosis and Heritage Digital Twins developed for the ARTEMIS Summer School 2026 in Hof, Germany. It builds on the concepts introduced in the session and uses examples from cultural-heritage restoration to explore how 3D digitisation, computational reassembly and human expertise can support the analysis, reconstruction and interpretation of fragmented objects. 

## Introduction

Digital restoration brings together Cultural-Heritage expertise, 3D digitisation and computational methods to support the documentation, analysis and reconstruction of cultural-heritage objects and sites. Within this context, a Heritage Digital Twin can provide a digital environment for working with 3D data, annotations, measurements and different restoration hypotheses.

This resource focuses in particular on **virtual Anastylosis and reassembly**: the computational reconstruction of fragmented or disassembled objects. It introduces the main challenges of this process, from uncertain fragment configurations and large search spaces to the different geometric and material properties of heritage objects. It also explores how 3D data is prepared for computational restoration, how fragments can be aligned and assembled, and how missing geometry can be predicted.

A central theme is the relationship between **computational methods and human expertise**. Rather than replacing the restoration expert, interactive approaches allow computational processes and expert decisions to work together. The resource also considers how services such as ARTEMIS can support this workflow and how web-based, accessible and collaborative environments can make digital restoration more practical for the Cultural-Heritage community.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the resource, learners will be able to:

* explain the role of Heritage Digital Twins in digital restoration, including how they support documentation, analysis and the exploration of reconstruction hypotheses;
* describe the main challenges and data requirements of virtual anastylosis and computational reassembly, including fragment uncertainty, large search spaces and the preparation of suitable 3D data;
* distinguish between manual, automatic and interactive restoration approaches, and explain how human expertise contributes to “user-in-the-loop” workflows;
* understand key computational concepts and collaborative challenges in digital restoration, including geometric alignment, shape prediction, prosthetic geometry, accessibility and web-based collaboration.

## Target Audience

The resource is aimed at students, researchers and professionals in **Cultural Heritage, archaeology, conservation, restoration and digital humanities**, as well as learners interested in 3D and computational methods. No advanced technical background is required.

## Intended Impact

The resource aims to provide a clear understanding of how digital and computational methods can support restoration. It highlights the importance of **task-oriented digitisation, human–computer collaboration and accessible digital tools**, while showing how Digital Twins can support the exploration, analysis and reconstruction of cultural-heritage objects.

### Requirements

No prior technical knowledge is required. A basic familiarity with archaeological objects, cultural-heritage restoration, or digital documentation is helpful but not essential.

### Suggested Learning Path

For learners who are new to digital restoration and Heritage Digital Twins, it is recommended to first watch the video resource [*Reconstruction of Archaeological Objects as a Digital Twin*](https://campus.dariah.eu/resources/hosted/reconstruction-of-archaeological-objects-as-a-digital-twin). The video introduces how the physical restoration and reassembly of archaeological objects can be transformed into a digital environment, where computational methods can be used to explore and test restoration hypotheses in a non-invasive way. The present text resource builds on these concepts by providing a broader introduction to digital restoration, virtual Anastylosis, 3D data, computational reassembly and the role of human expertise.

### Essential Terminology

* **Heritage Digital Twin:** Digital representation of a cultural-heritage object or site.
* **Digital Restoration:** Using digital and computational methods to support restoration.
* **Digital Anastylosis / Reassembly:** Virtual reconstruction of fragmented objects.
* **3D Digitisation:** Creating digital representations of physical objects.
* **Alignment / Registration:** Positioning digital fragments in relation to each other.
* **Shape Prior:** Geometric information that helps guide reconstruction.
* **User in the Loop:** Combining computational methods with human expertise.
