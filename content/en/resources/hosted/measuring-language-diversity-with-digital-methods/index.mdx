Understanding how languages structure expressions across different historical periods and linguistic traditions is a foundational challenge in textual studies. While traditional descriptive frameworks offer valuable insights, they capture only a fraction of this syntactic diversity. This resource introduces Patternization, an innovative framework and Python-based tool that integrates traditional text analysis and corpus linguistics with mathematical modeling to systematically examine syntactic variation across corpora.&#x20;

By modeling phrases as sequences of grammatical category labels, Patternization calculates and compares empirical, observed structural patterns against the full theoretical spectrum of potential constructions. Performing such exhaustive combinatorial analysis manually is virtually impossible; this presentation demonstrates how computational methods can surface subtle, systematic linguistic nuances that remain invisible through close reading alone. Designed for digital humanists, historical linguists, and corpus researchers, this resource provides both a conceptual introduction to syntactic pattern analysis and a practical computational tool for data-driven textual research.\
\
This presentation is part of the L*unch with Digital Humanities* lecture series, hosted by the Centre for Digital Humanities and Arts (DARIAH-IS/CDHA, [https://www.mshl.is](https://www.mshl.is/)) in Spring 2026.

## Learning Outcomes

After studying the resource, learners should be able to:

* explain how quantitative and combinatorial methods can be used to measure syntactic diversity and word order variation in historical corpora;
* understand the concept of "patternization" as an abstract template for analyzing syntactic constituents such as noun phrases;
* evaluate how custom Python scripts and annotated databases expand computational options beyond off-the-shelf software in Digital Humanities workflows;
* recognize the role of European research infrastructures and multi-institutional collaborations in supporting linguistic and humanities data.
