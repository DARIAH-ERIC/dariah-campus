This resource introduces the fundamental concepts of cultural heritage photography and discusses its role within the National Gallery of Iceland as a methodology for producing faithful digital reproductions of artworks, highlighting the technical challenges of capturing color fidelity and managing human visual perception. Particular emphasis is placed on how standardized digitization workflows, incorporating color target calibration, specialized software and international guidelines such as FADGI, Metamorfoze and ISO standards, support reliable, high-quality, and accessible environments for artwork conservation, scholarly research and public digital collections, while also underlining the importance of technical expertise and practical adaptability in establishing sustainable digitization practices.\
\
This presentation is part of the *Digital Humanities After Hours* lecture series, hosted by the Centre for Digital Humanities and Arts (DARIAH-IS/CDHA, [https://www.mshl.is](https://www.mshl.is/)) in Autumn 2025.

## Learning Outcomes

After studying the resource, learners should be able to:

* explain the importance of color fidelity in digital art preservation, recognizing how objective image capture supports artwork conservation, institutional access and research;
* identify international digitization standards and guidelines and compare their practical applications in museum workflows;
* understand the technical process of camera profiling and color calibration, including the use of color targets, profiling software and tethered capture environments;
* critique the challenges of subjective human color perception and object materiality, analyzing how studio lighting, surface gloss and visual illusions complicate faithful color reproduction.
